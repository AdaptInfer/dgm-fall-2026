abstract: >
  Lecture 6 built a single sigmoid unit as a Bernoulli likelihood and let autograd
  differentiate it. This lecture generalizes that recipe in two directions: from two
  classes to K classes via softmax and categorical cross-entropy, and from one unit to
  many via stacked fully-connected layers. Nonlinear activations are what make the
  stack more expressive than a single layer — enough to finally solve XOR — at the cost
  of a loss surface that is no longer convex. Backpropagation is then just the chain
  rule over that computation graph, written in matrix form.

## Section 4: Backpropagation

<!-- Slides 22-25. TODO: author -->

<!--
Points to cover:
- From Lecture 5's per-weight sums to matrix form: summing over paths IS a matmul
  delta_L = yhat - y;  delta_l = (W_{l+1})^T delta_{l+1} * sigma'(z_l)
- Worked XOR example: x -> [Linear(2,2), ReLU] -> h -> [Linear(2,1), sigmoid] -> BCE
  W1 = [[1,1],[1,1]], b1 = [0,-1], W2 = [1,-2], b2 = -0.5, x = (1,0), y = 1
  Forward: z1 = (1,0), h = (1,0), z2 = 0.5, yhat = 0.62
  Backward: dL/dz2 = -0.38, dL/dW2 = (-0.38, 0), dL/dh = (-0.38, 0.75),
  dL/dz1 = (-0.38, 0), dL/dW1 = [[-0.38,0],[0,0]]
  Hidden unit 2 is "dead" for this input, so its gradient is exactly zero
- Depth makes gradients fragile: the gradient reaching layer 1 is a PRODUCT of every
  layer's local derivatives. sigmoid' <= 0.25, tanh' <= 1, ReLU' in {0,1}
  -> vanishing or exploding gradients
-->
